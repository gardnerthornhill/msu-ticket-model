How the forecast works

The forecast is a straight-line model fitted to Mississippi State home games since 2023. One number does most of the work: the cheapest resale ticket on ticketdata.com, the get-in price, compared with the rest of that season. Opponent strength fills in the rest. The model is refit every morning, and every number on this site comes out of that fit.

What goes in

Four variables, all known before kickoff.

Opponent ranked in the AP Top 25
1 when the opponent was in that week's AP Top 25, otherwise 0.
Opponent Elo rating
CollegeFootballData's Elo rating for the opponent entering the game. Before an opponent has played, last season's final Elo stands in.
Opponent SP+ rating
Bill Connelly's SP+ rating for the opponent that season. Positive is better than average.
Get-in price, relative to the season
The cheapest resale ticket on ticketdata.com, compared with the median get-in price of that season's home games. Measured on a log scale, so doubling the price counts the same in any season.

How much each variable weighs

The price model starts from an average training game, 53,252 fans, and moves that number by each variable's weight times how far the game sits from average. The bars show the full swing each variable can produce: its weight times the gap between the lowest and highest value seen in training.

  • Opponent ranked in the AP Top 25weight 715, give or take 2,789 +715 seats
  • Opponent Elo ratingweight 1.95, give or take 6.86 +2,724 seats
  • Opponent SP+ ratingweight -48.36, give or take 121 -3,419 seats
  • Get-in price, relative to the seasonweight 6,405, give or take 1,819 +18,173 seats

Read the price bar and stop there. The three opponent variables overlap heavily (a ranked opponent has a high Elo and a high SP+), so their individual weights partly cancel and are not meaningful on their own. Judge the model by its out-of-sample error, below, not by the sign of any one weight.

The schedule-only fallback

How accurate it is

Each game was forecast by a model that had never seen it (leave-one-out). RMSE is the root mean square error in seats; R² is the share of the variation in crowd size the model explains, where zero means no better than the average.

How the variables were chosen

Every subset of up to three schedule variables was scored by leave-one-out error, and the smallest subset within 5% of the best was kept. The price variable was then added in two forms; the season-relative form won.

Where the data comes from

  • Get-in prices from ticketdata.com, recorded by hand. Training prices are the final price near game day; an upcoming game's price is whatever was last pasted in, and the date it was seen is shown on each game.
  • Schedule, attendance, AP polls, Elo and SP+ from CollegeFootballData, refreshed daily during the season.
  • Code and data are public on GitHub, including everything on this site as JSON.

What to keep in mind

  • Small sample: a couple of dozen games. Coefficients are rough; the leave-one-out numbers are the honest accuracy.
  • Attendance is the announced figure and is capped at Davis Wade Stadium capacity (60,417); sellouts flatten the top end, and intervals clipped at capacity no longer carry their nominal 80% coverage.
  • The get-in price is the final price recorded by ticketdata near game day, not a price observed weeks out.
  • Price levels shift season to season; the season-relative price feature exists for that reason.
  • schedule-only features were chosen by the same leave-one-out scores reported here, so the headline RMSE is optimistic; the runner-up subsets in the selection table bracket the realistic range.
  • Individual coefficients are not interpretable when the selected features are collinear (for example opponent Elo and SP+); judge the model by its leave-one-out error, not by coefficient signs.
  • price model uses a season-relative price, so it only becomes meaningful once a season has at least 3 priced games; predictions leave price model blank until then.

Questions people ask

Why does the ticket price matter more than who Mississippi State is playing?

The get-in price is set by thousands of buyers and sellers deciding, in real time, how much they want to be in the building. It already bakes in the opponent, the weather, the record, kickoff time and everything else. Opponent strength on its own explains less: within a season, the rank correlation between price and crowd size was about 0.93 to 0.95.

What does the 80% range mean?

It is a prediction interval. If the model's assumptions hold, the announced crowd lands inside that range four times out of five. It is wider for unusual games and narrower near the middle of the training data. Ranges that touch 60,417 are cut off there, because the stadium cannot hold more.

Why is every forecast capped at 60,417?

That is the largest crowd Davis Wade Stadium has announced; the official capacity is listed a little lower. A linear model can produce numbers above it, so the forecast and the range are clipped.

How often does the forecast change?

Every morning a job refreshes the schedule and ratings from CollegeFootballData, retrains the model and rebuilds this site. Get-in prices are pasted in by hand, because ticketdata.com blocks automated access; a forecast moves whenever its price is updated.

How good is it, honestly?

On the 16 games with both a price and an attendance figure, the price model's leave-one-out error is 2,967 seats (root mean square), against 5,635 for guessing the season average. That is a small sample; the weights are rough and will shift as games are added.
